# Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

We as members, contributors, and leaders pledge to make participation in the `craftframework` community a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## Our Standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment for our community include:

- Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people
- Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback
- Accepting responsibility and apologizing to those affected by our mistakes, and learning from the experience
- Focusing on what is best not just for us as individuals, but for the overall community

Examples of unacceptable behavior include:

- The use of sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind
- Trolling, insulting, or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
- Public or private harassment
- Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email address, without their explicit permission
- Other conduct that could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting

## Enforcement Responsibilities

Community leaders are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our standards of acceptable behavior. They will take appropriate and fair corrective action in response to any behavior they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

Community leaders have the right and responsibility to remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues, and other contributions that are not aligned to this Code of Conduct, and will communicate reasons for moderation decisions when appropriate.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all community spaces, and also applies when an individual is officially representing the community in public spaces. Examples of representing our community include using an official email address, posting via an official social media account, or acting as an appointed representative at an online or offline event.

## Enforcement

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behavior may be reported to the community leaders responsible for enforcement at:

[craftframework@becomechange.institute](mailto:craftframework@becomechange.institute)

All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly.

All community leaders are obligated to respect the privacy and security of the reporter of any incident.

## Enforcement Guidelines

Community leaders will follow these Community Impact Guidelines in determining the consequences for any action they deem in violation of this Code of Conduct:

### 1. Correction

**Community Impact**: Use of inappropriate language or other behavior deemed unprofessional or unwelcome in the community.

**Consequence**: A private, written warning from community leaders, providing clarity around the nature of the violation and an explanation of why the behavior was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.

### 2. Warning

**Community Impact**: A violation through a single incident or series of actions.

**Consequence**: A warning with consequences for continued behavior. No interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, for a specified period of time. This includes avoiding interactions in community spaces as well as external channels like social media. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or permanent ban.

### 3. Temporary Ban

**Community Impact**: A serious violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior.

**Consequence**: A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public communication with the community for a specified period of time. No public or private interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, is allowed during this period. Violating these terms may lead to a permanent ban.

### 4. Permanent Ban

**Community Impact**: Demonstrating a pattern of violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior, harassment of an individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of classes of individuals.

**Consequence**: A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within the community.

## Reporting and Support

The following project-specific procedures supplement the Covenant above. Community leaders here means the project maintainers handling a report, not a dedicated investigation service.

### Private incident reporting

Report conduct concerns privately to [craftframework@becomechange.institute](mailto:craftframework@becomechange.institute), preferably with the subject “Private conduct report”. Anyone affected by or witnessing an incident may report it. **Do not submit conduct, security, or confidential concerns through public GitHub issues, discussions, or the support chatbot.** Security concerns should also use private contact; omit credentials and exploit details from the initial message and ask how to share necessary details safely.

Share only what you are comfortable sharing and what is necessary to understand the incident:

- What happened, when and where (including time zone if known).
- Relevant account names, links, or redacted screenshots, and any witnesses.
- Whether harm is ongoing, immediate safety concerns, and any requested protective measures.
- A safe way to contact you, any communication/accessibility needs, and information you do not want shared.

Incomplete reports are welcome; reporters do not need to investigate or confront anyone. Do not send passwords, tokens, unrelated personal information, or confidential assessment/evidence records. Ask before sending sensitive attachments; maintainers can discuss a suitable method within available capabilities.

**Optional anonymous reporting:** you may omit your name or use a pseudonym and an email address that does not identify you. No dedicated anonymous reporting service is currently promised. Email providers, headers, attachments, and incident details may reveal identity; ordinary email cannot guarantee anonymity or secure transmission. Reports without a safe reply address can still be considered, but clarification, updates, or an outcome may be impossible. Limited evidence may limit available action; anonymity alone is not grounds to dismiss a report.

This mailbox is not continuously monitored or an emergency service. If there is immediate danger, seek appropriate local emergency help or platform safety assistance; do not wait for a project response.

### Confidentiality and non-retaliation

Incident information is shared only on a need-to-know basis with unconflicted maintainers or an agreed impartial reviewer who need it to assess safety, investigate, enforce a decision, or review an appeal. Do not forward reports to general support queues or publish them in issues, chat, or analytics.

Maintainers should discuss necessary disclosures with the reporter where feasible, minimize identifying details, and explain confidentiality limits. Absolute secrecy cannot be guaranteed: facts may identify someone indirectly, and limited disclosure may be necessary for a fair response, immediate safety, or applicable legal obligations. Do not promise legal privilege or immunity. Share only the necessary information, and inform affected people when safe and permitted.

Retaliation against anyone for a good-faith report, witness participation, requesting protection, or an appeal is prohibited. This includes intimidation, doxxing, pressure to withdraw, and adverse treatment for participating in the process. Report retaliation privately through the same channel; it is assessed as a separate violation. An unsubstantiated report is not by itself evidence of bad faith.

## Enforcement Process

### 1. Receipt, triage, and conflicts

Maintainers aim to acknowledge reports within **5 business days**, make an initial safety/process assessment within **10 business days**, and provide a decision or progress update within **30 calendar days** of receipt. These are targets, not guarantees: availability, complexity, evidence, and reviewer capacity may cause delays. When safe contact is possible, explain delays and the next expected update; aim to update open cases at least every 30 calendar days. If no acknowledgment arrives within 5 business days, a reporter may follow up privately using the same subject.

Anyone handling a case must disclose relevant conflicts (including being involved in the incident, close personal or professional relationships, or a stake in the outcome) and recuse from investigation and decision-making where impartiality is compromised. Recused people must not access case records beyond what is necessary to transfer the report or respond as a participant.

If a report concerns a maintainer or mailbox recipient, the reporter may send only a minimal request for an unconflicted contact before sharing details. The shared mailbox may be accessible to the person concerned; it is not an independent channel. An unconflicted maintainer should arrange handling, or, with the reporter's agreement, seek an impartial reviewer. No independent panel is guaranteed. If none is available, disclose the limitation, avoid a conflicted merits decision, and discuss safe next steps or a pause with the reporter.

### 2. Interim protective measures

While facts are being assessed, maintainers may remove harmful content, restrict project-space access, pause participation, or set temporary no-contact boundaries within project authority. Measures should be proportionate to the immediate risk, time-limited where possible, documented, and reviewed as new information arrives. They are protective steps, not a finding of misconduct. Communicate their scope, review date, and a private route to request reconsideration to affected people when safe, without exposing the reporter.

### 3. Factual and fair investigation

Unconflicted reviewers gather relevant accounts separately and distinguish established facts from allegations, disputed facts, and inference. Consider context, impact, available evidence, and its reliability; avoid assumptions based on identity, status, or popularity. Ask focused questions and retain only relevant information. Reporters may decline further participation; reviewers should explain how that could limit findings.

Give the person whose conduct is at issue a clear, privacy-minimized description of the concerns and a reasonable opportunity to respond, submit relevant evidence, or correct factual errors before a final decision where safety permits. State a response deadline that accommodates reasonable accessibility or availability needs. Immediate protective action need not wait for a response; document safety-based limits and offer a later response opportunity where possible.

No reporter or participant is required to meet, mediate, reconcile, accept an apology, or contact another participant directly. Communicate through reviewers; do not arrange forced contact or disclose private contact information. Decisions may proceed on the available evidence if someone does not respond, documenting uncertainty rather than treating silence as proof.

### 4. Proportionate decision and written outcomes

Apply the Correction, Warning, Temporary Ban, and Permanent Ban guidelines above, considering severity, impact, repetition, ongoing risk, and relevant previous violations. The ladder is not a mandatory sequence: serious conduct may justify a stronger sanction immediately. No violation finding or insufficient evidence are also possible outcomes. Any requested apology must not override the affected person's privacy or no-contact preferences.

Record the findings, relevant standard, reasons, uncertainties, and any measures. Notify the person subject to action privately in writing of the decision, sanction scope/duration, no-contact terms, conditions for return if applicable, and appeal instructions. No-contact restrictions do not prevent necessary case responses or appeals through the designated private reporting channel.

When safe contact is available, give the reporter a written closure summary and relevant safety information, without sharing another person's unnecessary private details. Explain if the case cannot be resolved or must be paused. Public moderation explanations should be minimal and must not expose incident records or identify a reporter.

## Appeals

Anyone subject to corrective action may appeal by emailing [craftframework@becomechange.institute](mailto:craftframework@becomechange.institute) **within 30 calendar days of receiving the written decision**. A reporter may also request review within 30 calendar days of receiving a closure summary. Use the subject “Private conduct appeal”, identify the decision, and explain any factual error, new evidence, procedural unfairness, conflict, or disproportionate outcome.

Appeals must be reviewed by an impartial person who was not involved in the original investigation or decision and has no relevant conflict. If no such maintainer is available, seek an impartial reviewer with the participants' agreement before sharing necessary records; do not invent an independent appeals body or let the original decision-maker decide the appeal. Explain any capacity limitation, delay, or pause and the next expected update.

The same acknowledgment and update targets apply to appeals. Reviewers may uphold, modify, or reverse the decision, or request further investigation, and communicate the reasons and outcome privately in writing to the appellant and relevant affected participants, with appropriate redaction. The appeal outcome is final within this process; new safety concerns may still be reported. Interim or existing safety restrictions remain in place unless an unconflicted reviewer changes them; an appeal alone is not retaliation or a new violation.

## Records and Transparency

Maintain only the necessary incident evidence, communications, findings, actions, and appeal records in access-restricted storage available to authorized, unconflicted reviewers. Do not place records in the repository, public tickets, shared support logs, or analytics. Use available account security and access controls, minimize copies, redact unrelated personal data, and avoid downloading unnecessary sensitive attachments.

Review access and retention at case closure and periodically thereafter. Delete unnecessary evidence and identifying details when no longer needed for safety, enforcement, appeals, or applicable obligations; retain only a minimal rationale and sanction history where still needed to handle repeated harm. Do not promise a fixed retention period or deletion from email providers/backups that the project cannot control. Participants may ask privately about access, correction, or deletion; maintainers should explain what can be done and any limits.

When capacity permits, maintainers may publish anonymised, aggregate summaries of report counts, broad outcome categories, and process improvements, never raw reports. Suppress small counts, distinctive facts, dates, or combinations that could identify people; if safe anonymisation is not possible, publish only general process lessons or no summary. Transparency must not compromise participant safety or confidentiality.
