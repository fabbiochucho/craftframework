## 3. The problem CRAFT addresses

Institutions often have policies, strategies, risk registers and ESG commitments, but their existence does not prove consistent application. Findings are recorded but not funded or owned; evidence is scattered; sustainability reporting is treated as communications; actions are marked complete when a document is produced. CRAFT provides a connective layer between diagnosis, planning, execution, evidence and review. It complements leadership, professional judgement, legal advice, the justice system and independent assurance — it does not replace them.

## 4. The end-to-end lifecycle

CRAFT's product lifecycle has four layers — **Assessment → Accreditation → Strengthening → Monitoring** — supported by this evidence chain:

Criterion → Finding → Root cause → Corrective action → Owner and deadline → Evidence → Review decision → Effectiveness check → Reassessment

**Rules that hold in the product (once deployed):**
- Marking an action complete does not close a CAP; closing needs an admin and approved, non-archived, unexpired evidence on every action.
- Closing a CAP never changes a score or marks a requirement compliant; the source criterion must be reassessed.
- A closed CAP can require an effectiveness review; a failed review reopens it. A CAP can also be reopened with a reason.
- The accountable owner cannot review the effectiveness of their own action; evidence uploaders cannot approve their own evidence.

## 5. CRAFT Governance Scorecard

Ten proposed CRAFT pillars (original CRAFT content, informed by — not equivalent to — the Nigerian Code of Corporate Governance, King, Mwongozo, OECD, COSO, ISO standards and the APRM):

1. Leadership, Board Effectiveness and Strategic Direction · 2. Ethics, Integrity and Organisational Culture · 3. Accountability, Transparency and Stakeholder Engagement · 4. Risk Management, Internal Controls and Assurance · 5. Financial Stewardship and Fiduciary Responsibility · 6. Operational Capability, Performance and Service Delivery · 7. People, Inclusion and Human-Capital Management · 8. Sustainability, Climate and Responsible Value Creation · 9. Data Governance, Cybersecurity and Digital Accountability · 10. Institutional Resilience, Continuity and Succession

**Scoring (methodology CRAFT-GS 0.1, provisional).** Each criterion is scored 1–5 (Absent, Initial, Defined, Implemented, Embedded and reviewed). A score without evidence counts at most 2. Not-applicable needs a reason and is excluded; unanswered criteria are reported, never scored. Pillar scores are weighted means; the overall score renormalises pillar weights (summing to 100) over answered pillars. Bands: Emerging (<40), Developing (40), Established (60), Advanced (80), Exemplary (90). The methodology has not been externally validated, and a CRAFT score is not an official score of any governance code. It is not a MOFI score either: the MOFI scorecard informs CRAFT, but no MOFI authority has been granted.

**Founder protection (innovation-support overlay).** For incubators, accelerators, startup competitions and innovation funders, four criteria are added: fair and transparent founder contracting; IP and confidential-information safeguards for every participant, not only winners; accessible grievance and dispute resolution with timelines; and demonstrable safeguard accountability, with breaches tracked as corrective actions. The aim is to make trust something institutions demonstrate, not something founders are asked to assume.

## 6. ESG & Sustainability Implementation Planner

Choose and adopt frameworks; each carries its publisher, category (disclosure standard, regulation, accounting standard, questionnaire, reporting framework), obligation (mandatory, mandatory where applicable, voluntary) and official link. Frameworks whose current version has not been verified are labelled so. Requirement titles are short paraphrases; CRAFT does not reproduce copyrighted standards.

**SEC Nigeria IFRS S1/S2 plan generator.** The SEC circular of 23 September 2026 requires public companies and significant public interest capital market operators to submit an implementation plan by **15 October 2026** covering eight minimum elements: (1) governance arrangements including Board oversight; (2) gap assessment against IFRS S1 and S2; (3) implementation roadmap and timelines; (4) data collection and reporting systems; (5) internal control and assurance arrangements; (6) capacity building and training; (7) expected first reporting year under the FRCN Roadmap; (8) key implementation challenges. CRAFT generates one editable plan per element with owner, timeline, milestones, activities, evidence, dependencies, completion indicator and a qualitative budget assumption (no invented costs). The SEC deadline is kept separate from the first reporting year: periods beginning on or after 1 January 2028 for public interest entities and 1 January 2030 for SMEs (FRCN SRG 1, §14 and §18).

## 7. Corrective Action & Evidence Hub

One organisation-level CAP register, fed by governance findings, ESG requirements, scorecard recommendations and quality-checklist gaps, each linked to its source. Records hold finding, severity, root cause, corrective action, expected outcome, owner, due date, resources, dependencies, numbered actions, evidence, verification, residual risk, effectiveness status and reopen count. A CAP raised from a sensitive governance finding references it without copying the sensitive text.

**Not yet built:** immediate correction vs preventive action as separate fields; merging duplicate findings with preserved provenance; escalation workflow.

## 8. ESG Report Quality Checklist

**Product principle: reward evidence of implementation, not the mere existence of paperwork.**

- **Reporting periods** belong to an organisation, which persists across years. Carry-forward copies owners and notes only; statuses restart and evidence must be reconfirmed.
- **Applicability engine.** The period start date, jurisdiction and entity category determine which packs apply and whether each check is mandatory.
- **Requirement classes.** M-REG (regulatory), M-STD (standard), M-GOV (governance mandate, e.g. MOFI scope), C-MAND (conditional), GP (good practice), CRAFT (internal evidence rule). Applicability is decided separately for each period.
- **Status rules.** Met requires linked evidence accepted by an authorised reviewer. Partially met and Not met require the gap. Blocked requires the blocker. Returned requires what is missing. Pending review requires linked evidence. Not applicable requires a rationale and approval by someone else. Exception approved is admin-only, earns no points and can never waive a mandatory requirement.
- **Scoring.** Weights are Critical 5, High 3, Medium 2, good practice 1. Met earns full weight and Partially met half; nothing earns points before evidence acceptance. The checklist also reports coverage, evidence acceptance, mandatory coverage and mandatory pass rate. These are proposed settings pending owner approval.
- **Readiness gate.** "Ready for approval" is never shown with an open critical check, an unresolved mandatory requirement, a pending N/A decision, unreviewed evidence or missing sign-off, whatever the score.
- **Sign-off.** The preparer cannot give final approval. Finalised periods are frozen snapshots and never silently recalculate; corrections create a revised version with reason, actor and time. Period lists warn when the entity basis or checklist version differs.
- **Action metrics.** Overdue action rate, on-time completion and effectiveness pass rate are shown alongside the score.

## 9. Roles and separation of duties (enforced)

| Rule | Where |
|---|---|
| Assessment creator cannot approve it | Governance assessments |
| Evidence uploader cannot approve it | Evidence registry |
| Only admins verify actions and close CAPs | Corrective Action & Evidence Hub |
| Accountable owner cannot review their own effectiveness | Corrective Action & Evidence Hub |
| Person marking a check N/A cannot approve it | ESG Report Quality Checklist |
| Preparer cannot give final sign-off | ESG Report Quality Checklist |

Roles: owner, admin, assessor, viewer; tenant isolation by organisation; audit log; field-level encryption of sensitive text. Multi-factor sign-in is not provided by the application.

## 10. Data, evidence and responsible use

Evidence should be relevant, specific, current, attributable and appropriate to the claim. CRAFT outputs do not establish certification, legal compliance, creditworthiness or investment suitability. Regulatory references carry their source and verification date; anything not confirmed is labelled "requires verification". Do not upload sensitive personal data without approval.
