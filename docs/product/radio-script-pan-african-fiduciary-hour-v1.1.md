Format: two-person radio dialogue. Hosts: **Olalu** (Lagos) and **Wanjiru** (Nairobi).
Every product claim below matches the CRAFT build on branch `feat/governance-planner-cap-hub`; nothing is described as live unless it exists.

**[AUDIO CUE: Upbeat African synth-jazz intro, 5 seconds, then under dialogue]**

**Olalu:** Ẹ ṅlẹ́ o, listeners, and welcome to The Pan-African Fiduciary Hour! I'm Olalu, live from Yaba, Lagos.

**Wanjiru:** And Jambo from Nairobi! I'm Wanjiru. Karibu to everyone across West, East and Southern Africa.

**Olalu:** Wanjiru, today's topic keeps founders, executives and board members up at night: the gap between what institutions *say* and what they can *prove*.

**Wanjiru:** We see glossy reports and impressive ESG statements, but too often it's paper governance. Institutions can name what needs fixing; turning that into a disciplined, daily process is where things break down.

**Olalu:** Kilode! Findings sit in consultant decks and never become funded, owned actions. Evidence is scattered across email and spreadsheets, and when an investor asks for proof, teams panic.

**Wanjiru:** And this timing matters. In Nigeria, the SEC's circular of 23 September 2026 asks public companies and significant capital market operators to file an IFRS S1 and S2 implementation plan by 15 October 2026. In Kenya, public interest entities report under IFRS S1 and S2 for periods from 1 January 2027. Ghana follows in 2027, and Nigeria's public interest entities in 2028.

**Olalu:** You can't copy-paste a European checklist into Lagos or Nairobi. Rules differ by country, so the tools have to know the difference.

**[AUDIO CUE: Show sting]**

**Wanjiru:** Which brings us to CRAFT — the Capacity Readiness & Fiduciary Assurance Toolkit, developed through DiBadili Institute.

**Olalu:** First, what it isn't: not a regulator, not a certification, not a credit rating, and not an audit opinion. It doesn't replace legal advice or the courts. It gives institutions the operational discipline to back up their claims with evidence.

**Step 1 — Assess**

**Wanjiru:** It starts with assessment. CRAFT's readiness assessment covers five tiers and twenty domains on a zero-to-five maturity scale, backed by a seven-point evidence matrix that runs from "no evidence" to "system-enforced".

**Olalu:** And here's the rule I love: the Paper Compliance Penalty. A beautiful policy manual can't inflate your score if the practice isn't there.

**Wanjiru:** There's also the new CRAFT Governance Scorecard. It has ten pillars, from leadership and ethics through data governance and resilience. Every answer is scored from one, "absent", to five, "embedded and reviewed". A score without evidence counts at most two. CRAFT is open that this methodology is still provisional.

**Step 2 — Plan**

**Olalu:** Then you plan. CRAFT's implementation planner covers IFRS S1 and S2, GRI, SASB, TNFD, the GHG Protocol, CDP and more, each linked to its official source. For Nigerian companies facing the SEC deadline, it generates the plan on the circular's eight required elements, with owners, timelines, milestones and evidence, and it downloads as a Word document.

**Step 3 — Act and prove: the Corrective Action & Evidence Hub**

**Wanjiru:** Step three is the engine. Every gap becomes a corrective action with an owner, a deadline and a root cause. Marking a task complete does not close it. Closing it needs approved, unexpired evidence, verified by an admin. And the person who uploaded the evidence can't approve it.

**Olalu:** Then CRAFT separates *doing* the fix from proving the fix *works*. You can require an effectiveness review after closure, and if the fix didn't work, the action reopens. Closing an action also never bumps a score on its own: the criterion has to be reassessed.

**Step 4 — Check the report: the ESG Report Quality Checklist**

**Wanjiru:** Before a sustainability report goes out, CRAFT runs a quality checklist that knows which country you're in. It covers Nigeria's SEC and FRCN rules, plus Kenya, Ghana, Zambia, Tanzania, Zimbabwe and Rwanda. A check can only be "met" when a reviewer has accepted the evidence. And however high the score, one open critical gap blocks approval.

**Olalu:** Score ninety-two out of a hundred, and still "approval blocked: two critical findings". That's honesty built into the software.

**The founder question**

**Wanjiru:** Olalu, we've had listeners ask about startup competitions and accelerators.

**Olalu:** Yes! Young founders need capital and mentorship, but they also need fair terms, informed consent and protection of their ideas. And that applies to every participant, not just the winners.

**Wanjiru:** So CRAFT now has an optional set of founder-protection criteria for organisations that fund or support innovation. They cover fair and transparent contracting, safeguards for intellectual property and confidential information, accessible dispute resolution with timelines, and accountability you can demonstrate.

**Olalu:** CRAFT can't replace strong laws or a working justice system. But it can help institutions fix what is within their control.

**[AUDIO CUE: Mid-show transition]**

**Wanjiru:** So what does this mean for Africa? Boards get visibility of real operational risk. Funders and investors see the same evidence the institution sees. And teams get clear ownership, which means no more last-minute audit panic.

**Olalu:** The ambition is simple: make trust something institutions demonstrate, not something founders and investors are asked to assume.

**Wanjiru:** Start with the free two-minute Readiness Check at craftframework.becomechange.institute.

**Wanjiru:** Thank you, Olalu, and thank you, listeners. Keep building with evidence and integrity.

### What was corrected from the earlier script

| Earlier claim | Correction |
|---|---|
| Scorecard evaluates "10 core dimensions" on an "Ad hoc → Reviewed and improving" scale | The 10-pillar CRAFT Governance Scorecard is now built, with a 1–5 rubric (Absent → Embedded and reviewed). The readiness assessment is separate: 5 tiers, 20 domains, 0–5 and a 7-point evidence matrix. |
| "Every response captures actual evidence references" | Evidence is self-declared in the assessment; it is verified in the evidence registry, and unevidenced scorecard answers are capped. |
| Root-cause analysis "required" | Root cause is now a CAP field; it is recorded, not enforced as a mandatory method. |
| Effectiveness checks "confirm the issue won't recur" | Effectiveness reviews are optional at closure, and a failed review reopens the CAP. They test whether a fix worked; they don't guarantee it won't recur. |
| Knowledge layer provides "templates" | No template library yet beyond the offline assessment template. |
| "Lowering the cost of capital" | Stated as an aim, not a proven outcome. |
| 10-stage lifecycle presented as the product | The product lifecycle is Assessment → Accreditation → Strengthening → Monitoring. |
