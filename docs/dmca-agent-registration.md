# DMCA Designated Agent — What You Need To Do

I can't submit this registration myself — it requires your legal name/organization identity and a $6
payment to the US Copyright Office. Here's exactly what to do and why it matters less than the checklist
implied for your current sites.

## Does this actually apply to you right now?

The DMCA safe-harbor protection a designated agent provides only matters if your platform **hosts public,
user-generated content that other people could upload and that a third party could claim copyright over**
(the classic case: an image-hosting site, a forum, a social platform).

I checked both sites:
- **CRAFT's Data Room uploads** are private, tenant-scoped compliance documents — each organization only
  uploads and sees its own evidence, never shared publicly. There's no public surface where a stranger's
  copyrighted image/content could appear.
- **The Institute site** has no user-upload feature at all — it's a static marketing site.

So neither site currently has the exposure the checklist's "$150,000 per stolen image" scenario describes.
**This becomes relevant if CRAFT's Data Room, or any future feature, ever exposes uploaded content publicly**
(e.g., a public case-study gallery, a public document library). Worth registering proactively if that's on
the roadmap; low priority if not.

## If you decide to register anyway (cheap insurance, 10 minutes)

1. Go to the US Copyright Office's DMCA Designated Agent Directory: the registration is done through their
   online system at copyright.gov (search "DMCA Designated Agent Directory Public Search System" — I'm not
   linking directly since the exact URL structure changes and I don't want to send you to a stale path).
2. You'll need: your legal entity name (DiBadili Institute / Become Change Institute, whichever is the
   actual registered legal entity), a physical address, a phone number, and a designated agent email address
   (a role address like `dmca@becomechange.institute` is standard — don't use a personal inbox).
3. Pay the $6 registration fee.
4. Once registered, add a line to your site's footer or a dedicated `/dmca` page stating your registered
   agent's contact, e.g.: *"Copyright infringement notices under the DMCA should be sent to our designated
   agent at dmca@becomechange.institute."* I can add that page/section once you've actually registered and
   have the real contact details — publishing a DMCA policy referencing an unregistered agent would be
   worse than having none, since it implies protection you don't actually have yet.
